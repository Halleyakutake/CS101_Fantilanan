##  $ shoe_size: num  6.5 9 8.5 8.5 10.5 7 9.5 9 13 7.5 ...
##  $ height   : num  66 68 64.5 65 70 64 70 71 72 64 ...
##  $ gender   : chr  "F" "F" "F" "F" ...

R assigned the gender column the type character (chr), which is what I expected because gender is text made of letters. If a numeric column like shoe_size was accidentally imported as character because of a stray non-numeric value, calculations such as mean(), sum(), and cor() would fail or give wrong results. Sorting would also use alphabetical order instead of numeric order, so “10.5” would come before “9.0”.

Checking both head() and tail() is good practice because head() only shows the first rows, while problems often appear at the end of a file. For example, extra blank rows, a stray footer or note, or a row with a missing or misplaced value at the bottom would not show up in head() alone. tail() confirms that the whole file was imported correctly, including the last rows.

The males have a higher mean shoe size (about 10.96) and a higher mean height (about 71.46) than the females (about 7.86 and 65.68). This shows a pattern where the group with bigger shoe sizes is also taller.

Yes, the dataset supports a relationship within each gender group, since the correlation inside each group is positive (about 0.5). But it is weaker than the overall 0.78, which means gender is an important factor that must be accounted for separately. Part of the overall relationship comes from the males being both taller and having bigger feet.
